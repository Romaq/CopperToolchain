### The answer to your question

- **SSH cannot forward UDP.** Its `-L` and `-R` forwards carry TCP only, which is why your reverse tunnel works for TCP and stops there.
- **Your target game needs UDP.** Hytale's official server guide says the game uses QUIC over UDP, listens on port 5520 by default, and does not need TCP forwarding.
- **The workaround:** wrap each UDP datagram in a small frame, carry the frames through a single TCP connection (which SSH can carry), and unwrap them at the other end. That is `udp-bridge.py`.

```
player --UDP--> [relay: bridge ENTRY] --TCP inside the SSH reverse tunnel--> [home: bridge EXIT] --UDP--> game
```

The relay runs the entry end, which listens for player UDP. Home runs the exit end, which hands the datagrams to the game. The tunnel is the reverse SSH connection you already know how to open.

### What's in the kit

- **`udp-bridge.py`:** the bridge, with `entry` and `exit` modes. It uses only Python's standard library and is about 150 lines. It keeps datagram boundaries, supports several players over one connection, reconnects by itself, and drops datagrams (with a warning) instead of queueing them when the tunnel is congested.
- **`udp-probe.py`:** your "means to verify it works". An echo server plus a client that reports loss, duplicates, reordering, damaged or merged datagrams, and round-trip time, ending in `PASS` or `FAIL`.
- **`udp-selftest.sh`:** runs everything on one machine with no SSH or network, so you can see the whole system work and read it as it runs.
- **`udp-over-ssh.md`** (for `docs/runbooks/`): the step-by-step runbook, a six-rung verification ladder, security settings (a key that can open only one port), troubleshooting, and a "done means" checklist.

### What I tested, and what I found

- The self-test passed 10 runs out of 10.
- **The verifier can actually fail.** I fed it a deliberately naive bridge that treats UDP as a plain byte stream. It reported merged datagrams (a 64-byte packet that arrived as 1,840 bytes) and `FAIL`. That is the failure the framing exists to prevent, and a verifier that never fails would prove nothing.
- Reconnection works: starting the entry end before the exit end, and restarting the exit end mid-run, both recovered by themselves. The allow-list option blocked a client it should have blocked.
- Paced traffic (1,000 datagrams at 200 per second) arrived with zero loss.
- **A known limit:** bursts of 200 or more datagrams sent in the same instant occasionally lose a few, because they overflow a socket buffer. Bursts of 150 or fewer were clean in all 8 runs. Real game traffic is paced, so this only matters in stress tests. I found it because an early version of my self-test failed once, and I made the test realistic and the limit documented rather than hiding it.
